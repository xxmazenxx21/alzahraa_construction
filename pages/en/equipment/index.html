Our Fleet

A self-owned fleet — because delivery certainty starts with the machine on site.

Al Zahraa has, since the late 1990s, chosen to own its heavy machinery rather than rely on rented equipment for its core road and bridge work. That decision — a large capital commitment sustained project after project — is the reason our sites don't wait, our sequences don't slip, and our clients don't hear the word "unavailable" when we quote a schedule.

Owned, not rented. A rented paver arrives late, arrives serviced by someone else, and leaves when its next job calls. Our pavers arrive on the day our programme says, tuned by our own mechanics, and stay for the full duration of the works.

Fleet built for the work we actually do. The machines were specified around road and bridge construction — asphalt pavers, tandem and pneumatic rollers, tracked and wheeled excavators, wheel loaders, motor graders, aggregate crushers, transport, and batching plants. Nothing in the yard is idle inventory.

Maintained continuously, not seasonally. Every machine sits inside a scheduled maintenance regime — hours-based inspections, oil and filter cycles, hydraulics audits, undercarriage checks — logged on machine, not in a filing cabinet.

Why This Matters

What a self-owned fleet actually gives you on a project.

The advantages are practical, measurable, and — over the life of a corridor programme — decisive.

  • 01

    Schedule certainty

    The paver you tender with is the paver that shows up. No queuing behind another contractor's job, no last-minute reshuffles when the rental company overbooks.

  • 02

    Sequencing flexibility

    Because the fleet lives in our yard, we can rebalance machines across concurrent sites within days — accelerate the section that's ahead of programme, redirect capacity to the one that's behind.

  • 03

    Consistent quality

    The same operators run the same machines across projects. That accumulates into a level of finish — screed marks, compaction consistency, subgrade tolerance — that changing hire crews cannot match.

  • 04

    Cost transparency

    We own the depreciation curve. That means we can quote long-duration programmes on their real machinery cost, not on rental market swings during the delivery window.

Workshop & Maintenance

An in-house workshop is why the fleet stays available.

Every machine we run passes through a scheduled maintenance regime managed by our own mechanics — not outsourced, not reactive, not seasonal.

Preventive first, reactive second. Every machine has a maintenance schedule keyed to engine hours, not calendar dates. Filters, oils, hydraulics, greasing points, undercarriage components, cutting edges — all logged against the individual machine's history, not against a general job card.

On-site mobile maintenance. For active corridor projects, mobile service units travel to site so a machine doesn't lose a day being trucked back to the yard for a scheduled item. The yard becomes the destination for overhauls and major rebuilds only.

Consumables and spares in stock. The workshop carries filter, belt, hose, and hydraulic-seal inventory for the makes and models on our fleet register. A single-machine failure doesn't cascade into a schedule impact because the replacement part isn't waiting on a shipment.

Records on request. Prospective clients auditing our capability can request maintenance-record extracts against the machines proposed for their programme — an option most contractors are not able to offer.

The People on the Levers

A machine is only as good as the operator running it.

A paver laying to the wrong crown, an excavator over-trimming a subgrade, a grader whose blade sits half a degree off — these are operator problems, not machine problems. That is why our operator development sits inside the same programme as our machine investment.

  • Certified operators, tracked hours

    Every operator carries an internal certification against the machine class they run. Certification is renewed on hours, and cross-training is standard, so a crew doesn't collapse when one operator is on leave.

  • Safety-first culture

    No production target overrides a safety judgement. Pre-shift equipment checks are a hiring standard, not a slide in a training deck.

  • Long-tenured crews

    Many of our operators have been with the firm for more than a decade. That continuity is a quality factor — machine-specific muscle memory doesn't come from a two-week orientation.

How We Mobilise

From the yard to the corridor, on the day the programme says.

Moving a fleet is its own discipline. Ours is planned as tightly as the works themselves.

  1. 01

    Pre-mobilisation inspection

    Every machine leaves the yard only after a checklist — hydraulics, undercarriage, cabin systems, safety devices — signed off by workshop and dated.

  2. 02

    Low-bed transport

    Heavy machines move on our own low-beds and trailers, on permitted routes, with escort where axle loads require it.

  3. 03

    Site commissioning

    On arrival, machines are commissioned by a workshop technician before the operator takes over. This eliminates the "arrived-broken" argument that plagues rented equipment.

  4. 04

    Continuous availability tracking

    Machine status — active, standby, in maintenance — is visible to the project manager in real time. Rebalance decisions happen on facts, not on phone calls.

Sustainability

Owning the fleet also means owning its emissions.

Environmental performance is a specification item on modern public-sector infrastructure. Our fleet is managed with that specification in mind.

Fleet renewal on a defined cycle. Older, less efficient units are retired against a rolling renewal plan — not held on the books for accounting reasons and run past their useful life.

Fuel consumption tracked per machine. Consumption is logged per unit and reviewed monthly. Machines whose consumption drifts away from their expected envelope get workshop attention before their emissions do.

Reduced idle time. Operator training explicitly includes shut-off discipline. On a large site, avoided idle time is the single largest fuel — and emissions — saving available to us.
